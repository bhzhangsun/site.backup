To understand the collective sexual repression of today's youth, we must first understand the social structure we grew up in.

## I. Compressed Modernity: The Background of Our Generation

For thousands of years before the Reform and Opening-Up, Chinese life was shaped by the dual constraints of clan and morality. People lived in clustered communities, bound by personal connections and clan kinship. You were born into a village surrounded by relatives; marriage was a matter for two families, even two villages. In this structure, people were not atoms — they were nodes in a network. Your loneliness was held by others, your desires were seen, your marriage was arranged.

After the Reform and Opening-Up, and through the 2010s when industrialization reached its peak, two major transformations occurred: **urbanization and marketization**.

Marketization demanded that work become a universal norm. Competitive hiring made relationships between people tense — you were no longer a brother working alongside you in the production team, but a competitor. Urbanization turned clustered village life into atomized families in apartment buildings. You could live next door for three years and never know your neighbor's name. The great collective collapsed, replaced by rows of locked security doors.

By the 2010s, China's industrial system had matured. The generation that grew up during this time — the post-80s and post-90s — found that **the experience of their parents had become completely obsolete**. Your father couldn't teach you how to survive in an internet company; your mother couldn't advise you on renting an apartment in a first-tier city, job-hopping, or managing social insurance. Family relationships shifted from support systems to burdens.

Crucially, the phenomenon of "left-behind children" grew increasingly common among the post-80s and especially post-90s. This continued into the 2000s in rural areas, and remains common today. Parents' life experience could no longer support their children's growth. Family bonds grew thinner. Most left-behind children grew up never having truly felt loved.

How can someone who was never properly loved in childhood learn to love others? How can they dare to ask for love?

This is the first layer of our collective predicament: **many of us were never given the tools to love.**

## II. The Loss of Social Skills: We've Forgotten How

I only truly understood this after attending a classmate's wedding in Xinjiang.

The Uyghur people are natural dancers and singers. But that's not talent — it's culture. In their culture, social interaction is part of daily life. Gatherings, song, dance, expressing emotion — these are ingrained habits. They don't need to learn "social skills" as a separate discipline; they've been immersed in a social environment since childhood.

And us?

In traditional Chinese culture, awareness of social interaction has always been relatively淡漠. Social skills, frankly, were only passed down in aristocratic and literati families. In *Dream of the Red Chamber*, Jia Baoyu's way of dealing with people — that was family tradition. Ordinary families, especially rural ones, taught their children: "Speak less, do more," "Don't cause trouble when you're out."

Our generation has it worse. Left-behind children had no one to teach them. City children were locked in their rooms doing homework. The cultivation of social skills was left almost entirely to self-study. But intimacy is the most complex form of social skill. If you can't even talk to a stranger, how can you build an intimate relationship?

So we see a strange phenomenon: online, everyone cries out for love and connection; in real life, men and women sit across from each other, scrolling through their phones, unable to say a single word.

## III. The Impact of Western Liberal Culture: The Collapse of Trust

From the study-abroad wave of the 80s and 90s, to the Korean Wave, to the invasion of Western feminism — our cultural environment has undergone violent upheaval in just a few decades.

China's humanities education deserves special mention here. It is, frankly, **a complete mess**. Trust between men and women has been thoroughly destroyed.

On one hand, the traditional moral system has collapsed. The rule of law can't keep up, moral constraints have weakened, and traditional marriage has become a joke — cheating, divorce, property disputes — these stories flood social media every day. Who can still believe in marriage?

On the other hand, Western feminism has been transplanted onto Chinese soil without local digestion, turning into a gender war on social media. Dialogue between men and women has become mutual accusation and attack. Men think women demand too much; women think men aren't good enough.

In this environment, **trust has become a luxury.**

## IV. The Cost of Sex

### The Male Predicament

Before a certain point in time, sex for ordinary men was almost entirely dependent on marriage. And the cost of that was the cost of marriage.

From the earliest "marry a man, marry a meal ticket," to requiring a bride price, to needing a car and an apartment — this happened in just forty years. With housing prices remaining sky-high, the cost of marriage has become something ordinary men simply cannot afford.

It's not that these men don't want to marry. **Many of them simply cannot afford to.**

What are the alternatives? Sex workers, mistresses — these have become options for some. Of course, some still marry, but the cost remains high. Not all men can pay it, because for most men, even basic living expenses have become a problem in just the last five years.

### The Female Predicament

Women's sexual needs go unmet for two core reasons.

First, **sex is often not prioritized as a primary need for women.** This has both physiological and social conditioning causes. Women are taught to be "reserved" and "not too forward," so many won't even admit to having sexual desires.

Second, **media-driven de-masculinization has taught women that masculinity equals danger.** On social media, negative news about men is amplified infinitely — domestic violence, cheating, PUA, femicide. These stories are real, but algorithmic amplification creates the impression that all men are potential threats. A natural distrust arises between the sexes.

Meanwhile, the media promotes idealized male images, raising women's standards. What they see online are carefully curated images; when they meet ordinary men in real life, they feel these men "don't measure up."

This creates a vast demand gap.

The gap is filled in various ways. Male sex workers serve a market. Women's sexuality is inherently a resource, and affluent third parties naturally take their share. But the remaining ordinary women **become lambs living in fantasy** — neither daring to take the step nor able to lower their standards, living vicariously through celebrity worship, TV dramas, and shipping fictional couples.

## V. Conclusion: The Mismatch of Sexual Resources in an Atomized Era

Sexual repression is not the problem of any one person or any one gender. **It is the result of layered structural collapse in the social relations of our era.**

When people are stripped away from clans, villages, and collectives, becoming isolated atoms, our social instincts do not disappear. We still crave intimacy, love, and the touch of body and soul.

But the reality is: we lack the ability to build intimate relationships, we lack the trust to sustain them, and we lack the resources to obtain them.

**The mismatch between the expectation of social instinct and the reality of sexual resources has led to this collective sexual repression.**

It is no one's fault. It is the problem our era has given to our generation.

And we can only learn to solve it ourselves.
